CONCEPT MAP - 03_advanced (README tier: ORANGE Advanced)
==========================================================
Isolation + AR: frame posture and configuration surfaces, Nexus and rifts,
rooms by kind (STATIC and CAPABILITY), the View* read family, ward
policies, and checkpoint/load. 18 lessons authored, 01-18 sequential.

HOUSE RULE - STRINGS, NOT ENUMS
  Demos pass STRINGS for every enum-shaped argument: existence="unique",
  with_space_type("static"), with_system_state("dynamic"),
  set_new_policy("outbound_only"), with_nexus_frame_mode("single").
  Every one of those setters is typed Union[Enum, str] and normalizes
  through EnumHelpers.convert_enum_and_check. Measured convention across
  the corpus: 113 string uses vs 8 enum uses. Enum references survive ONLY
  where the enum itself is the subject (the RiftSpaceType doc-drift check
  at lesson 10, the auto()/.value footgun at lesson 16).

  AND HERE IS THE REASON IT IS A RULE AND NOT A STYLE (measured
  2026-08-02, after `.value` assertions went red on the owner's run):
  MELDER'S ENUMS ARE NOT CONSISTENT ABOUT `.value`.
      SystemState     auto()  -> .value is an INT (automatic == 1)
      Policies        auto()  -> .value is an INT (block_all == 3)
      Existence       auto()  -> .value is an INT
      RiftSpaceType   str     -> .value == "static"
      NexusFrameMode  str     -> .value == "single"
  So `SomeEnum.member.value` gives you the mode name for two of them and
  a number for the other three. `.name` is the string form EVERYWHERE.
  Reading a property back? Use `.name`. Passing one in? Use a literal.
  Never reach for `.value` unless you have checked which kind it is.

  15 what_you_cannot_see (THE READ SURFACE REPORTS ITS OWN BLIND SPOTS.
     Absence is AMBIGUOUS - melder says so: "absence means 'not visible
     to this rift' OR 'not present', INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM OUTSIDE". So
     it ships the disambiguator: describe_spell_missing_sections /
     describe_conduit_missing_sections / describe_missing_surface. THE
     WITHHELD-SECTION PROBE reports the NAMES of sections you cannot
     read and never the bodies - "this is how you tell hidden from empty
     WITHOUT THE CONTENTS" - which is why it is safe to ship to a
     low-authority room. visible/missing are COMPLEMENTS; either alone is
     half an answer. ANTI-FOOTGUN: frame_name is an ASSERTION, not a
     selector - wrong name raises instead of answering about something
     else. For an agent this is the difference between "no source
     binding exists" and "I was not shown the source binding")

ARC D COMPLETE (lesson 16)

  17 taking_a_checkpoint (CRYSTALLIZER FOLLOWS AETHER'S LADDER, NOT
     NEXUS'S - same "ORDERING RULE: THE CONFIGURATION MUST BE ACTIVATED
     FIRST" in the same capitals. So it is 2 vs 1: caller-driven
     activation is the HOUSE RULE and Nexus is the exception. Guess
     aether's ladder on a new subsystem and you are right more often
     than not. CrystallizerConfigurationBuilder is the most generous in
     the library - build/finalize/activate, ONE TERMINATOR PER RUNG -
     while AetherConfigurationBuilder offers only build(). THE
     CHECKPOINT: create_checkpoint(...) -> an ID, and the ID is the whole
     handle for list/describe/analyze/verify. Checkpoints ACCUMULATE,
     they never overwrite)

  18 loading_it_back (CREATED vs CACHED are two places a checkpoint can
     live; flush_checkpoint() is SEAL-THEN-SHIP - one verb, two legs,
     DIFFERENT GUARANTEES. THE REMOTE LEG IS LENIENT BY DEFAULT: "a
     successful return does NOT prove the remote received anything". The
     one deliberate exception to melder's never-substitute rule, and the
     reason is sound - a network you do not control must not fail your
     local checkpoint. SECOND WARNING: the cache is FIFO bounded, so a
     flush CAN EVICT an older cached checkpoint as a side effect. "I
     flushed it" is not "it is still there" - list_cached_checkpoint_ids
     is the cheap check. Eviction is a SIDE EFFECT; deletion is a
     DECISION, with its own verb)

SUBSYSTEM ACTIVATION DIVERGENCE - IT IS 3 TO 1
  Aether            caller activates the config, THEN aether.activate()
  Crystallizer      same
  MutationResearch  same
  Nexus             enable() finalizes FOR the caller  <- lone exception
  Caller-driven activation is the house rule. Owner note 2026-08-02:
  "activate means something specific" - the divergence is Nexus, not the
  verb.

THE THROUGH-LINE (named at lesson 09, teach it forward)
  Melder NEVER conflates "this exists" with "this is live". Every
  subsystem splits it in two and only the names change:
      09  config  frozen        / activated
      10  nexus   is_configured / is_enabled
      11  rift    is_registered / is_active
  When you meet an unfamiliar melder object, find its two bits first.
  Presence never implies liveness.

SECOND THROUGH-LINE (visible by lesson 12) - MELDER NEVER SUBSTITUTES
  When melder cannot do the thing you asked, it says so. It does not do
  an adjacent thing quietly:
      08  validate() RAISES rather than returning False
      13  static has no meld() - authority by ABSENCE, not by refusal
      14  weak binding RAISES rather than degrading to strong
  Corollary for readers: a melder call that returns normally did the
  thing you asked, not a near miss.
